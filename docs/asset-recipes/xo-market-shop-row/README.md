# XO3 — Two-Bay Desert Shop / Food-Stall Row

An original, procedurally authored modular desert-market structure: a two-bay row made
of one entrance bay (small shop) and one open serving bay (food stall), built in
Blender 5.2 from scratch. Geometry, material assignment and the wall/opening construction
are hand-authored; only the surface maps are third-party CC0 inputs.

This is the **second** authored market structure in this series. It is not a variant of,
and shares no generator code or mesh data with, `xo_market_authored` (the larger market
hall). The shared idea is only the medium: adobe/mudbrick massing on a dead-flat base,
timber accents, hand-thrown ceramics, and a laundry line of awning cloth.

Read `PROVENANCE.md` for the complete input and licence record, and
`RECIPE_NOTES.md` for the source-member-by-source-member build recipe.

## The structure

Overall footprint **8.92 x 6.71 m** (x 8.92, y 6.71), ground to parapet top **4.07 m**.
A 780 mm thick adobe envelope, battered (sloped) on the exterior faces, with a
78 cm-thick front wall split into two bays and a 300 mm internal divider.

**Bay A — entrance / small shop (x -3.90 .. 0.30, eaves 3.05 m)**
- A **0.50 m deep recessed entrance**, threshold step up into the wall plane, with stone
  reveal linings, timber jambs and a 5-plank door standing 58 degrees open.
- A 6-slat timber hood over the door, cloth over it.
- A **flared window** — 380 mm deep, splayed so the outer aperture is *smaller* than the
  inner, the way a real mud-plaster reveal is finished — with a 4-board shutter open 104
  degrees, a stone sill, a stone head, a 7-board reveal lining and a shelf inside.
- A small **tapering niche** 300 x 760 mm, 380 mm deep, splayed the other way (outer
  *larger* than inner), with a timber shelf and two jars.
- A 5-course buttress at the west end, and a taller west end wall with a 3.4 x 2.6 m
  service door on the rear.

**Bay B — food stall (x 0.30 .. 3.90, eaves 2.82 m, deliberately lower)**
- A 2.48 x 1.28 m **serving hatch** with a 2.72 x 0.16 m dressed stone counter on
  plastered piers, with a scupper notch cut into the counter slab, corbel brackets, and
  a 1.14 m stile-and-rail shutter propped open above.
- A 1.9 m wide **slatted timber awning**: ledger fixed into the facade over the hatch,
  3 rafter pairs, 2 purlins, an outer rail, 22 slats per span with a 15 mm droop, two
  116 mm posts on 340 mm stone footings with knee braces, iron tie rods, a drip strip,
  rope lashings, and a sagging striped cloth over the eastern bay.
- A 1.6 m wide timber **price board** hung on chain from the awning ledger.
- Interior: a firebox hearth with a stone top, a fire grate over emissive embers, a
  3-tier shelf, prep table with bowls, hanging chilli strings, onion braids, a pan, a
  strainer, a water jar and a flour sack.

**Roof** — 230 mm thick mud screed decks, cambered (a shallow ridge per bay) and stepped
in height between the bays. Bay A gets a plain 250 mm parapet with a 70 mm coping; bay B
gets crenellated merlons. Below the eaves: 9 timber corbels, fascia boards, a roof hatch
with a propped cover, a pigeon loft with a perch, ledge and a sleeved entry hole, jars, a
bucket and stacked timber.

**Service** — rear wall of 1.8 m ground-level windows with a grid grille, a roof
downspout with three straps into a stone soak box, a hose bib with a coiled hose, an iron
utility box, a yard tap into a water butt, a lean-to over the rear yard, a roof ladder,
and a firewood log stack.

**Boundary** — the row is closed on all four sides: front return stubs on the west and
east ends (capped with stone and a drip course), and rear boundary walls with a gate
post/head on the east. A 0.40 m wide channel drain runs along the front, and a 0.24 m
apron trodden around the building. The ground is an extruded closed solid 0.30 m thick —
not a floating plane.

### What is on the counters and shelves

62 pottery members (lathed, 24 segments, 9 profiles, with 0.9 mm seams and 1.6% oval
distortion), 39 fruit and 28 leaf pieces in lathed bowls, baskets and crates, 23 straw
and grain items, 20 rope lashings, and the 14-burner brass lantern.

## Reproducing it

```bash
cd /var/folders/pt/hwgh5hrj1p909_r6ns6bkggm0000gn/T/opencode/xo_market_authored_pass3
BL=/Applications/Blender.app/Contents/MacOS/blender

$BL -b --factory-startup --python src/prep_textures.py      # -> textures/*.png (24)
$BL -b --factory-startup --python src/build_shop_row.py     # -> MEMBERS.txt, shop_row.{blend,glb}
$BL -b --factory-startup --python src/build_collision.py    # -> shop_row_collision.{blend,glb}
$BL -b --factory-startup export/shop_row.blend  --python src/render_shop.py   # -> renders/*.png
$BL -b --factory-startup export/shop_row.blend  --python src/diag.py
```

`render_shop.py` and `diag.py` operate on the already-open scene, so the blend must be on
the command line. Run time: textures ~10 s, build ~25 s, collision ~2 s, renders ~90 s,
diag ~5 s (all CPU).

The build is **deterministic**: two consecutive runs of `build_shop_row.py` produce
identical vertex and face counts and byte-identical GLBs. Getting there required beveling
each authored member *before* the weld rather than after — Bevel's solver is not
reproducible on a single 28k-vertex merged mesh, and a whole-mesh bevel was silently
rejected on roughly 40% of runs, changing the face count.

## Units and orientation

Metres, Z-up, origin at the centre of the building footprint on the ground plane.
The **front facade faces -Y**; a consumer walking in from +X/-Y sees the door and the
counter. Exported with `export_yup=True` (glTF convention), so in a glTF viewer the
asset is Y-up and the front faces -Z.

## Honest weaknesses

These are real, and none of them is fixed by saying it clearly.

1. **Not watertight, and not claimed to be.** 3,830 non-manifold edges, of which 2,750 are
   inside the donor lantern (a third-party mesh I did not clean) and 1,080 are the
   hand-authored meshes. Those 1,080 are T-junctions where members abut — plaster on
   plaster, timber framing running into a wall face — plus the edges of the wavy
   solidified cloth sheets. Nothing used for collision is derived from them — the collision set is 12
   explicit hand-placed boxes — so this is a mesh-hygiene and future-editing issue, not a
   runtime issue. A voxel-remesh or a proper weld pass would fix it; both would change
   the authored silhouettes, which is why it was left alone.
2. **The donor lantern is decimated.** The original CC0 brass lantern is 12,742 triangles,
   20% of the entire asset. It is decimated to 2,908 (body 0.22, chain 0.22, link 0.40).
   At 1280x720 and 1.8 m away it is indistinguishable, but the chain links and the
   pierced body filigree are noticeably simpler than the source. This is a deliberate
   budget decision and it is a real loss of detail.
3. **The collision set is a gameplay ground-floor set, not a full envelope.** The awning
   canopy and its slats are omitted because 2.07 m of clear height under them is walkable.
   All roof elements — decks, parapets, merlons, corbels, the pigeon loft, the roof
   clutter — are omitted. The rear lean-to and the gate head beam are omitted. The front
   boundary stubs are folded into the front-wall boxes, so those two boxes over-block by
   about 0.50 x 0.58 m at each front corner. A consumer climbing onto the counter or the
   awning ledge will not collide with the thing they are standing on.
4. **A three-point light rig, not a sun.** The facade faces -Y, the sun is placed at
   +Y-Z, and the deep 500 mm door recess, the window reveal and the stall interior receive
   almost no light from it. The renders add four *render-only* practicals (two point
   lights inside the recesses, one in the yard, one large rear bounce) to make those
   volumes legible. They are **not** part of the exported asset and a real scene must
   supply its own interior lighting — or the recesses will read as black holes, which is
   exactly what the first render pass showed.
5. **The textures are faked at the seams.** UVs are box-projected in metres, which gives
   consistent texel density across every member and no stretching — but it also means a
   single wall face has a different texture phase on each of its faces, and no brick
   course continues around a corner. At 1280x720 from 3 m this is invisible. It would
   not survive a 1.5 m close-up.
6. **Roof decks are `XO3_MudScreed`, not the wall's mudbrick.** A mud-brick wall would
   never have a brick-laid roof, so the deck is a lime-and-mud screed with chopped straw.
   This is a deliberate correction of an earlier pass where the roof read as planking from
   the air. It is the right call, but it does mean the roof is a *nominally* different
   material from the walls directly beneath it.
7. **Small-scale believability is uneven.** The three roofs' worth of clutter, the
   crates, the baskets and the produce are convincing at medium and aerial range. Up
   close (shot 01) the loaves are lathed ellipsoids and the chillies are bent tubes. At
   the distances this is meant for, that is an acceptable trade; the 1280x720 gameplay
   test is shot 01, and nothing in it reads as a placeholder, but nothing in it reads as
   a hero prop either.
8. **No LOD2.** The reference structure in this series has one; this does not. The asset
   is 55,890 triangles, which is comfortable for a mid-range mobile budget but is not
   defended at 30 fps. Nothing here has been profiled in an engine.
9. **This has never been loaded in an engine or a glTF viewer.** Every number in this
   document comes from Blender. The GLB was not re-imported and re-validated, only
   written and hashed. It has not been shown to render correctly anywhere except inside
   Blender.
